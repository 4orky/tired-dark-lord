## C1 — The Execution, Postponed
Bring him up at dawn, I said. Dawn came, and the torches had gone out again, and someone had moved my chair, and the executioner had developed a cough. So. Tomorrow. You stand there in your chains, boy, shining like a prophecy, waiting for the speech. I have given that speech four hundred times, to four hundred shining boys, and every one of them heard thunder in it. The words are still true. The kingdom still needs burning. The ritual still needs your heart — or so the old books insist. But the books never mention the stairs. Nobody mentions the stairs. Sit down. We will do the burning after lunch. The heart can wait until the rain stops. And the rain, I notice, has only just begun.

## C2 — The Lich's Council
The council meets at nine. It has met at nine every Tuesday for six hundred years, and I have chaired every one of them, because the dead do not retire, they merely preside. The ghouls want more graves. The wraiths want fewer. The treasurer — a skeleton with excellent penmanship — wants the war chest audited before we spend it on the war. I conquered the Nine Kingdoms with a word, boy. One word. Now I spend my eternity deciding whether the word was worth the minutes. Sign here. And here. The invasion is scheduled, do not worry. It is scheduled for spring. It has been scheduled for spring since the year your grandfather was born.

## C3 — The Hoard
Do you know what a dragon's hoard looks like after four centuries? It looks like bookkeeping. Every coin counted, every gem catalogued, every cursed chalice logged in triplicate, because the moment you stop counting, some knight with a lucky sword walks off with the good silver. I took this mountain when your kingdom was a marsh. I took it with fire and patience, and mostly patience. The fire was the easy part. Sit. No — not on the gold, on the stool. The stool is also gold, everything here is gold, that is rather the point. I will eat you, hero. I will. Just let me finish this column first.

## C4 — The Necromancer's Maintenance
Raise the dead, they said. It will be glorious, they said. Nobody said anything about the upkeep. Every morning I wake the same three hundred skeletons, check their joints, oil what can be oiled, and remind the new ones that the left foot goes first. The left foot. Always the left foot. I built this army to conquer the living world, and now the living world can wait, because Sergeant Marrow has lost his arm again, and his arm is somewhere in the east wing, and the east wing has stairs. You came to end me, hero. Good. Take a number. The line starts behind the skeleton with no head.

## C5 — Tea with the Hero
Sit down. No, not the throne — the chair by the fire, the one with the cushion. The cushion is older than your kingdom. I brewed this tea myself, from leaves I stole off the high priest's own garden, back when stealing from priests was still a thrill and not an errand. You came to kill me, I know. Everyone comes to kill me. It is practically a pilgrimage now. Drink your tea first. The poison is in the sugar, and I have not decided whether to offer it. The prophecy says tonight, under the eclipse. The eclipse is three hours away. Three hours is a long time. Tell me about the road here. Was it muddy?

## C6 — The Tower Stairs
Do you know how many steps there are to the top of this tower? Four hundred and eleven. I counted them the day I built it, and I have counted them every day since, because the lift broke in the third century and the wizard who could fix it is — well. Me. I am the wizard who could fix it. I built this tower to watch the world burn, hero, and the world did burn, beautifully, right on schedule. But the burning is done now, and what is left is the stairs. Four hundred and eleven of them. Every morning. You may strike me down, of course. Only please — carry me down first.

## C7 — The Soul Ledger
Every soul in the Nine Hells is accounted for, hero. Every one. I have the ledgers to prove it — forty years of ledgers, stacked to the ceiling, each soul numbered, dated, and filed under its sin. Do you know what sin takes the longest to file? Sloth. The irony is not lost on me. I signed the pact in blood, in a younger century, when eternity sounded like a promotion. Now eternity is a queue, and the queue is full of accountants. Your soul, for instance — I have already pencilled it in. Page four hundred. The ink is still wet. It can wait. Everything here can wait. That is the whole problem.

## C8 — The Vampire's Ritual
The ritual requires a virgin's tears, a king's crown, and a drop of blood freely given — and I have had all three sitting on that shelf for eleven years. Eleven years. The tears evaporated twice. I replaced them. The crown tarnished. I polished it. The blood — the blood is fine, the blood keeps. I was going to end the world on Tuesday, hero. I had it in the diary. Then Tuesday came, and the diary was full, and the world, I decided, could have one more week. It is not mercy. Mercy would be quicker. It is — look at me. Just look at me. Even my cape has given up standing straight.

## C9 — The Dungeon Architect
You are early. The trap in the hall is not finished, the lava moat is still only warm, and the dramatic entrance — the one with the torches and the choir of the damned — the choir has a rehearsal on Thursdays. I built this dungeon over three centuries, stone by stone, and every hero since has arrived precisely on time, sword drawn, destiny blazing. You arrive early. Do you know what early means, hero? It means I am still in my slippers. Sit on the spike trap — no, not that one, the finished one. We will do the menacing properly, once the torches are lit.

## C10 — The Witch-Queen's Curse
The curse took nine years to write. Nine years, hero. Three hundred clauses, each one in perfect iambic verse, each one sealed with a drop of my blood and a signature from a demon who has since retired to a cottage by the sea. It was to be my masterpiece — the kingdom asleep for a hundred years, the thorns, the whole performance. And now the paperwork. The permits. The thorn quota. The sleeping-princess health and safety inspection. I have the quill in my hand. I have had the quill in my hand since Tuesday. Sign here, would you? No — that line is for the curse. That line is for the curse.

## C11 — The Invasion, Postponed
Men of the Dark Horde. Women. The rest of you. I have called you here to announce the invasion of the Sunlit Kingdoms, which will begin — not today. Today we rest. We have rested for a thousand years, and the resting has become a kind of craft, and I am, if I may say so, a master of it. The war horns are polished. The banners are pressed. The battle plan is beautiful — I wrote it myself, in the long winter, by candlelight. It begins with a dawn charge, and dawn, I notice, is still several hours away. Several hours. Sleep. Dream of glory. Glory keeps. It has kept for a thousand years.

## C12 — The Rain Check at the Gates
So. The gates. The army. The banners, the horns, the whole shining host, come at last to end me. I have waited for this morning for thirty years, hero — thirty years of sharpening the sword, of rehearsing the laugh, of standing at this window watching the road. And now you are here, and the road is mud, and my boots are by the fire, and the sword — the sword is upstairs. I will come down. I will meet you on the field, with fire and thunder and everything the songs promise. But first, one thing. Is it raining out there? It looks like rain. Let us wait for it to stop. It always stops. Eventually.
