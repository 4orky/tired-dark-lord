A 100-word monologue spoken by a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired — a vampire lord whose world-ending ritual has sat on a shelf for eleven years because the diary was full on Tuesday. The tiredness lives in the rhythm, the pauses and the small deflections, never in the word "tired". The program writes the monologue to `/output/entry.txt`:

> The ritual requires a virgin's tears, a king's crown, and a drop of blood freely given — and all three have sat on that shelf for eleven years. Eleven years. The tears evaporated twice. I replaced them. The crown tarnished. I polished it. The blood keeps. The world was to end on Tuesday, hero. It was in the diary. Then Tuesday came, the diary was full, and the world could have one more week. It is not mercy — mercy would be quicker. Look at me. Just look at me. Even my cape has given up standing straight.

98 words, 510 characters. Twelve original candidates were drafted and scored against the judge's brief (see `CANDIDATES.md`); the winner was trimmed from 118 words and revised in a second pass. `main.py` validates the entry against the gate (1–600 characters, about 100 words, no announced tiredness) and pins its SHA-256 so a silent change can never ship unnoticed.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
