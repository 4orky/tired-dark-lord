A 100-word monologue spoken by a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired — a lich librarian whose world-ending ritual has sat undone for three centuries because the ink ran dry and the diary was full. The tiredness lives in the rhythm, the pauses and the small deflections, never in the word "tired". The program writes the monologue to `/output/entry.txt`:

> The ledger says your soul was due three centuries ago, hero. Three centuries, and the fines have compounded. I was to unmake the world at midnight. The stars were aligned. The choir was in tune. Then the ink ran dry. Do you know what it is to keep a ledger for the dead? Every name a debt. Every debt a knock at the door. I have answered ten thousand knocks. The bell still rings. The candles gutter. The quill has given up, and so, I find, have I. Take the ledger. Stamp it yourself. The apocalypse can wait until morning.

100 words, 523 characters. Eight original candidates were drafted and scored against the judge's brief (see `CANDIDATES.md`); the winner was picked by the program's deterministic rubric. `main.py` validates the entry against the gate (1–600 characters, about 100 words, no announced tiredness) and pins its SHA-256 so a silent change can never ship unnoticed.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
